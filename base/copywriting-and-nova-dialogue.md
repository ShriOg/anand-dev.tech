## 2. Final tagline decision

Three candidates were proposed back in Section 8 of the brief:
1. *Turning curiosity into products.*
2. *Building things that shouldn't exist yet.*
3. *Ideas become reality here.*

**A fourth, stronger candidate has now emerged organically from the renders themselves** — the Mindset header card:

> **"How I think. How I build. Why I keep going."**

This one has an advantage the other three don't: it wasn't invented for marketing, it surfaced from inside the world itself, which makes it feel authored rather than written-for-the-hero-section. It's also structurally nice — three short clauses that map loosely onto Mindset / Workbench+Creations / Window, i.e. the whole arc compressed into one line.

### Hero headline + subtext combinations, for testing

**Option A — "How I think" as headline**
> ## How I think. How I build. Why I keep going.
> I'm Anand — founder of MenuNova, builder of Nova AI, and someone who'd rather ship something small than perfect something that never leaves the workbench.

*Read: confident, a little philosophical, leads with process over product. Risk: reads slightly abstract before the visitor has seen anything yet — it's a stronger line *after* walking the room than as the very first thing seen.*

**Option B — "Ideas become reality here" as headline, "How I think" as a secondary echo**
> ## Ideas become reality here.
> How I think. How I build. Why I keep going.

*Read: clearer immediate promise up front, with the more reflective line riding underneath as texture rather than the whole weight of the first impression. Lower risk for a first-time visitor who hasn't explored yet.*

**Option C — "Building things that shouldn't exist yet" as headline**
> ## Building things that shouldn't exist yet.
> Founder of MenuNova. Builder of Nova AI. Currently arguing with an Arduino about whose fault the bug is.

*Read: punchier, more personality-forward, pairs well with the workbench/founder-humor tone already validated ("Caffeine > Bugs," "v0.3 RIP"). Slightly less elegant as a closing-line echo than Option B.*

**Recommendation, if a single pick is needed:** Option B — it gives a first-time visitor an immediate, concrete promise ("Ideas become reality here") while still surfacing the more reflective line ("How I think...") in the same breath, rather than choosing one mood over the other. The reflective line can then recur verbatim at Mindset (where it already lives in the render) as a satisfying callback rather than a repeat.

### ✅ LOCKED — Final hero copy

> **Ideas become reality here.**
> How I think. How I build. Why I keep going.

Optional third line beneath, still open: *"Explore the workshop →"* or *"Every project starts with a question."*

**Why B won, for the record:** it's the only option that uses the workshop's own structure rather than competing with it. Line one answers "what is this place?"; line two answers "who is this person?" — a clean hierarchy a cold visitor can actually parse. It also creates a deliberate callback: the second line reads as ordinary hero copy on arrival, then becomes a *discovered artifact* when the visitor reaches Mindset and finds it again on the wall, no longer copy but something the room itself believes. And critically, it stays warm and curious rather than tipping into rebellious startup-founder energy (Option C) — curiosity, not ambition, is the workshop's actual core quality, which should inform tone choices downstream (Nova's voice, remaining copy, future art prompts) too.

**No job title in the hero.** No "Full-Stack Developer," no "Founder," no buzzwords — the workshop itself proves those things; the hero copy doesn't need to state them.

## 4. Discovery Layer — Hidden Note Sequence (v2)

**Why v2 exists:** the first attempt at this sequence read too polished/wise — out of step with the voice already validated everywhere else in the renders ("Caffeine > Bugs," "v0.3 — RIP," Nova roasting his own project, "It's not supposed to work yet"). This version leans into the same human, slightly chaotic, occasionally funny register — most notes set up a sincere thought, then undercut it.

1. **Entrance**, inside the floorplan sketchbook — *"Every project starts as a question. This one started with way too many."*
2. **Behind a pinned sketch** — *"Current status: [ ] genius [x] curious. Good enough."*
3. **Workbench**, near a failed prototype — *"This was supposed to work. It had other plans."*
4. **Hidden inside the "v0.3 RIP" drawer** — *"Reminder: a failed prototype is still a prototype. A folder named 'final_final_v7_REAL.zip' is still a cry for help."*
5. **Tucked under the Arduino** — *"I spent three hours debugging this. The power cable wasn't plugged in. Nobody needs to know that."*
6. **Creations shelf**, behind MenuNova — *"People think products start with confidence. Most start with: 'wait... what if...'"*
7. **Empty fourth slot** — *"Reserved. For the thing currently keeping me awake at 2AM."*
8. **Mindset wall** — *"Focus on impact. Not because it's noble. Because nobody remembers perfectly optimized irrelevance."*
9. **Hidden behind a diagram** — *"Build. Ship. Learn. Repeat. Panic is optional."*
10. **Tucked into a notebook** — *"Every project teaches something. Usually after insulting your intelligence first."*
11. **Near Nova** — *"If you're reading this, Nova definitely helped. Nova claims otherwise."*
12. **Window, final discovery** — *"The workshop is still under construction. Honestly, I hope it always is."*

**This also supersedes the original closing line in Section 12.** The wow-moment text at the Window should now read:

> *The workshop is still under construction.*
> *Honestly, I hope it always is.*

The second line turns the ending from a statement into something a person would actually say — closer to a curious builder talking to another human than to portfolio copy signing off.
