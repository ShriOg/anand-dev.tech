## Style baseline (carried over from the validated render — keep consistent)

Stylized premium 3D diorama — Pixar environment quality, Studio Ghibli warmth, Monument Valley simplicity, Apple product-reveal polish. NOT photorealistic, NOT low-poly/game-asset, NOT plastic-toy/Roblox, NOT flat cartoon shading. Warm wood, soft matte materials, brass accents, paper textures. Restrained two-temperature palette: one warm dominant light source, one small cool accent. Shallow depth of field, ~35–50mm feel, sharp center, soft edges implying the room continues beyond frame.

## Prompt v2 (revised after first-pass review)

**What changed from v1:** the first pass nailed the mood, the personal-artifacts copy, and the style baseline — but the background corridor was too resolved. It showed a long hallway straight through to Nova himself, clearly visible with his glowing eyes, rather than staying a soft tease. v2 pulls that back so only a warm glow hints at what's ahead, consistent with the same "tease, don't resolve" principle that worked in the Nova's Desk render's window cue.

Keep everything from the v1 direction below — the doormat, the sticky notes, the sketchbook table, the dawn light through the open door — **except**:

**Background — soften further:** remove the clear, in-focus view down a long hallway to Nova. Instead, the space beyond the entrance threshold should fall into soft shadow and heavy blur almost immediately — just a warm, indistinct lamp-glow somewhere in the depth of the frame, with no resolved figures, no recognizable Nova silhouette, no readable furniture. If anything is sensed back there, it should read as "warmth exists further in," not "I can see exactly what's down there."

**Depth of field:** push the falloff harder than v1 — sharp focus should stay on the entrance threshold and the notebook table in the foreground; anything past roughly 2–3 meters into the room should be a soft wash of warm color, not a legible scene.

## Original v1 prompt (for reference)

### Scene-specific direction

**Setting:** the entrance to the workshop — 3.0m × 2.5m, two windows letting in dawn/golden-hour light (per the floorplan spec). This is the very first thing a visitor sees, so the mood should feel like an invitation, not a wall of detail to parse.

**Mood:** Wonder. Quieter and more open than Nova's Desk — fewer objects, more soft morning light, a sense of "something is about to begin" rather than "here's everything at once."

**Key elements:**
- Warm golden-hour light pouring through two windows or an open doorway, low-angle, long soft shadows
- A small reading nook or entry table with a stack of sketchbooks, loose papers, one or two unfinished diagrams pinned or resting nearby
- One glowing sticky note — the first discovery-layer note in the treasure-hunt sequence (Sec. 13a) — placed somewhere visible but not centered, inviting a closer look
- A hint of greenery (one plant, simple, not lush) catching the window light
- Optional: Nova himself glimpsed here too, small and distant, since he should be sensed throughout the room (Sec. 18) — if included, keep him secondary, not the focal point of this corner

**What NOT to include:** tools, wires, gears, anything that reads as "workbench" — that clutter belongs to the next zone. This space should feel calm, open, and a little unfinished — like curiosity before any building has started.

**Continuity cue (per the forward-tease rule):** somewhere in the background, softly out of focus, the warm lamp glow of the Workbench should be just barely sense-able — a hint of warmth in the depth of the room, not a resolved second scene. This is the same "tease, don't resolve" principle that worked well in the Nova's Desk render's window cue.

**Composition:** wide enough to feel like an entrance/threshold — slightly more open framing than Nova's Desk, since this needs to read as "the start of something" rather than an intimate close-up.
